# Client discovery examples (illustrative, synthetic and unvalidated)

These cases are scenario design examples for UX, consultant discussion and staged test development. They are **not observed client results**, confirmed decision-engine outputs, approved cost estimates or reference labels. S0 reviewers should author the final expected golden cases independently.

## A. Nairobi wholesaler with fragmented digital operations

**Initial profile:** Established SME; distribution/retail; goal = reduce order errors and customer follow-up delays. Context: WhatsApp orders and spreadsheets are reconciled manually with accounting data; follow-up owners change by shift. Intended user wants to automate quotations within 90 days and has a budget ceiling but no verified vendor quotes.

**Sample interview sequence:** start profile → TEC core questions identify manual transfers → evidence probe asks concrete past-week re-entry frequency → DATA prompts establish which spreadsheet is authoritative → MET asks whether quotation-to-order conversion is measured → GOV asks about customer records and approved AI sharing → budget and staff capacity entered last.

**Expected advisory logic (requires S1 confirmation):** Do not begin with an AI chatbot. Examine authoritative customer/order records, map handoffs, record baseline, define owner and data-handling safeguards. Potential actions A14, A04, A06 and/or A07 depending on actual gates. **Do not claim a revenue uplift or a fixed monthly cost** merely from the self-report.

**Silverspark follow-up:** Verify actual order flow, sample records and reconciliation frequency with the owner; propose a paid workflow/data diagnostic only if it adds value. Potential service line: Digital Systems and eventually AI Transformation.

## B. Sole proprietor starting AI literacy

**Profile:** One-person service provider, mainly WhatsApp plus paper records; no AI experience, no external cash budget, low staff capacity. Motivation: draft customer responses faster.

**Expected advisory logic:** Neutral language (“you”) and understandable low-cost steps, not penalties for missing boards or committees. Review data-sharing rules, suggest a narrow non-sensitive learning exercise and one output-checking checklist. The best initial recommendation might be **training, not implementation**. Avoid a vendor integration, expensive setup or “pilot-ready” claim.

**Silverspark follow-up:** Respect no-spend preference; recommend self-help if no engagement value. Potential service: AI Enablement only when the client wants external help.

## C. Enterprise piloting consequential AI decisions

**Profile:** Larger organisation, some well-managed systems and staff with AI experience; intended workflow supports credit or hiring decisions. Technical extended answers show incomplete test sets and no documented fallback.

**Expected advisory logic:** High aggregate maturity does not override the high-impact gate. Specialist legal/security/domain review and human decision authority precede pilot continuation. No “safe for production” approval. Relevant advisory actions may include A20, A21, A10 and A18 depending on exact supported evidence.

**Silverspark follow-up:** Ask for workflow risk classification, model evaluation approach, human oversight authority, incident response and current architecture; consult competent specialists rather than giving legal advice.

## D. Respondent selects “I am not sure” throughout

**Profile:** The person lacks access to operations and governance information; 14 scored core answers unknown. The exact interval is **0–100** with **0% coverage** and no maturity band. The product must still give verification-first guidance, not suppress the report or fabricate an average score.

**Silverspark follow-up:** Find the business decision-maker or operator who can verify the main goal, available records, workflow and AI use.
